Phentermine: the proven appetite suppressant

FDA-approved, clinically effective, and one of the most prescribed weight loss medications in the country. When willpower needs backup, phentermine helps your brain and body get on the same page.

Book Weight Loss Consult (682) 283-6976
What Is Phentermine

A short-term tool that makes long-term change possible.

Phentermine is an FDA-approved appetite suppressant that works by stimulating the release of norepinephrine in the brain. This reduces hunger signals, increases satiety, and gives you the breathing room to build sustainable habits around food and movement.

It's not a magic pill -- and Brittany will be the first to tell you that. But when combined with nutritional guidance, activity, and sometimes GLP-1 therapy, phentermine can break the cycle of cravings and overeating that makes weight loss feel impossible.

Phentermine has been prescribed for weight management since 1959. It's one of the most studied and well-understood weight loss medications available, and Brittany uses it as part of a comprehensive, individualized plan -- not as a standalone prescription.

Quick facts

  • FDA-approved since 1959 -- one of the longest-standing weight loss medications
  • Works by reducing appetite and increasing satiety through norepinephrine release
  • Typically prescribed for 3-6 months as part of a broader weight management plan
  • Oral tablet or capsule -- taken once daily in the morning
  • Prescribed and monitored by Brittany Adams, APRN, FNP-C with regular check-ins

Is Phentermine Right For You?

Good candidates vs. contraindications

Good Candidates

  • BMI of 30+ (or 27+ with weight-related health conditions)
  • Struggled with appetite control and portion management
  • Ready to pair medication with lifestyle changes
  • No uncontrolled cardiovascular conditions
  • Looking for a short-term tool to jumpstart sustainable habits
  • Want provider-monitored support, not a quick-fix online Rx

Not Appropriate For

  • Uncontrolled high blood pressure or heart disease
  • History of stroke or pulmonary hypertension
  • Hyperthyroidism or glaucoma
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Currently taking MAOIs or certain antidepressants
  • History of substance abuse (phentermine is a controlled substance)
